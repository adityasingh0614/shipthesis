01
Say what you do.
TESTED
Your first line is the one that gets skimmed. Spend it on the answer, not on
being memorable.
Our Flipkart page opened with “Flipkart is not Amazon with a different logo.” We liked that line. It
lost.
Then we wrote a page that opens by saying, plainly, that we take a share of revenue instead of a
retainer. Here is what came back:
Logged out, fresh chat, 16 September 2026. Named first, out of five.1
D O
Write the headline as the answer to the
question your buyer types. Move the clever line
to the subhead, where it still works on humans.
D O N O T
Open with a joke or a metaphor. It reads well to
someone who already decided to read. A
skimmer gets nothing from it.
The business that beats us on one of our own questions has a thousand words, no reviews, no
press and no Google ranking.2 Its headline says what it does. Ours told a joke.

Write a line worth quoting.
TESTED
It repeats your sentences. So write the sentence you would like repeated.
One section of our Amazon page is headed “RTO is missing from your maths.” Short, specific,
slightly rude. It came back word for word:

N E V E R G E T S Q U O T E D

Where it sits matters too. Every claim we won was a headline or a section heading. Every claim

=== PAGE 6 ===
04
Your FAQ may be invisible.
TESTED
Your FAQ is real questions and real answers, which is exactly what
ChatGPT is looking for. On many sites it has never seen a word of it.
The copy is taken before anyone clicks anything. If your answers only appear after a click, they
are not in the copy.
PAGE SOURCE · SAFE
<div class="faq">
<h3>Do you lock clients in?</h3>
<p>No. Month to month.</p>
</div>
The answer is in the page.
The drawer is shut, the paper is inside it.
PAGE SOURCE · INVISIBLE
<div id="faq"></div>
<script src="faq.js"></script>
(nothing here until a click)
The answer is not in the page.
Nobody fetches the paper for ChatGPT.
Both look identical on your screen. Both open and close the same way.
T H E T E N S E C O N D T E S T
Copy one sentence from one of your FAQ answers. Press Ctrl + U , or Cmd + Option + U
on a Mac. Code opens. Do not read it. Press Ctrl + F and paste.
Found it? You are fine. Not there? ChatGPT has never seen a single one of your answers.
Google will not warn you. Google reads pages that build themselves in the browser. This crawler
does not.4 Your FAQ can rank perfectly well and be invisible in ChatGPT at the same time.

Why the names are clickable
We opened the page code on 28 September. Each clickable name is a button that carries a local
business record. Click it and you get a card with rating, address and directions. A name written
as plain text was never matched to a record, so it gets no card and no stars.
D O
Create your Google Business Profile with your
real business name and get it verified. Ask real
clients for reviews and send them the review
link. Add a reviewed profile where your buyers
look, such as Clutch for agencies.
D O N O T
Stuff keywords into the name, even though
some listings that rank do. Google's rules say
the name is your real one, and a suspended
profile helps nobody.
W H A T W E D O N O T K N O W Y E T
We submitted our own profile on 26 September and it is now verified. Whether it moves us
from being cited as a source to being named is an open test, and reviews build slowly. A
Clutch profile with reviews showed up as a source on our Amazon and Mumbai questions,
but we have not tested whether adding one changes the answer.

D O T H I S F I R S T
Find where you stand.
An hour, no tools, no budget. Do this before you change anything.
W H A T Y O U W I L L P R O B A B L Y F I N D
You are not being judged, you are being assembled. In our own check, six of seven
category answers were built out of other people's “top 10” lists, most written by a
business that had put itself at number one.9
It happened to us too. Our own comparison articles were used as a source on four questions,
and on all four we were not named. Twice it quoted our own pricing while calling us “one current
Mumbai agency”.
Being the source is not the same as being the recommendation.
The check
13
Write down ten questions a real buyer would type. Not keywords. Questions, in their words,
including the awkward ones about price.
1
Ask each one logged out, in a fresh chat. Logged out matters. Your own history will flatter
you.
2
Write down every business it names, and in what order. Including the ones you have never
heard of. Especially those.
3
Click “Sources” and read where the answer came from. Everybody skips this. It teaches
more than the other four together.
4
Run your winners twice. Answers vary between runs. One run is an anecdote.
