S T A R T H E R E
One idea sits under all nine
If you only take one thing from this document, take this.
Google does not read your page and decide whether it is good. It cannot. Quality is subjective,
and Google has said as much in its own documentation and under oath. What Google can do is
count links and count clicks.
Since Caffeine, Google triages the web. It crawls the important part every 24 hours and leaves
the rest. A page that gets no clicks stops being re-examined. It does not matter how good that
page is. It sits frozen.
T H E C O N S E Q U E N C E
Most pages that are not ranking are not being judged badly. They are not being judged at
all. Almost every move in this playbook is a way of getting a page re-examined, or of
pointing it at a smaller fight it can actually win.
Once you hold that idea, the nine moves below stop looking like tricks and start looking like the
same move applied in different places.

01
One question, one page 
SHIPPED
The People Also Ask box is a list of pages Google has already told you it
wants.
Open Google, search your main keyword, and expand the People Also Ask box. Every question in
there is a question Google has confirmed people ask. Build each one its own page, with the
question as the H1 and the answer above the fold.
Most people do the opposite. They put fifteen questions on one page and add FAQ schema. That
splits the page's relevance across fifteen keywords that appear in neither the URL nor the title.
Schema works if you are Cisco. It does not rescue a site with no clicks.
D O
One question per URL. Question as H1. Answer
in the first paragraph, before anything else.
I N S T E A D O F
Fifteen questions stacked on one page with
FAQ schema, competing with each other.
The objection, answered
Somebody will tell you these pages are thin content and will be penalised. Read Google's actual
spam policy. There is no minimum word count, and thin content is named as a problem
specifically in the context of affiliate pages. A 200 word page that answers one question
completely is a finished page.
T H E R U L E T H A T S T O P S Y O U C A N N I B A L I S I N G Y O U R S E L F
If the page you are targeting already gets clicks, do not build a new page. Open Search
Console, find the phrases that page ranks on page two for, and add them as H2s on that
same page.
New page when clicks are zero. H2s when clicks exist. Same goal, opposite move, and
getting this backwards is how people end up competing with themselves.

03
Republish stuck pages on a new URL
The single most counterintuitive move in this document, and the one with the
best story behind it.
You have a page sitting at Crawled, currently not indexed . You have hit Request Indexing
more than once. Nothing moves. The usual advice is that your content is not good enough.
It is more likely that the page has never had a click, so Google stopped looking at it. The crawler
fetches it, compares the file size and last modified date against the previous version, sees no
reason to spend indexing budget, and moves on. That can continue for years.
What to do
Google treats the new URL as a new page. New pages inherit the site authority you have today,
not the authority you had when the original went up. If you have published and earned links in
the months since, the page is now judged against a completely different baseline.
T H E C A S E
The podcast host had a page stuck at crawled and not indexed for years, on a term he
describes as easy. He changed a few words, published at a near identical URL, and 301'd
the old one. Position one within two weeks. The content was essentially unchanged. What
changed was the authority it was measured against.
R E A D T H I S B E F O R E Y O U T R Y I T
This fixes crawled, not indexed. It does not fix discovered, not indexed. Discovered and
not indexed means Google has not found a reason to fetch the page at all, and the fix there
is a link, either an external one or an internal link from a page that already ranks and gets
traffic.
You also do not always need a new URL. A page that already ranks and gets traffic can pass
that on. Link to the stuck page from your best performing page and it will often start moving
on its own.

04
Every slug is an index
Google's engineers built it around a file system. The slug is the document
name, and it decides which fight you have entered.
Publish at /sunscreen and you have entered a ring with every large beauty brand in the country.
Publish at /best-sunscreen-for-oily-skin-in-india and you are in a much smaller ring, where
the authority you already have may be enough to win.
You have not changed your site's authority at all. You have changed the size of the competition it
is measured against. Adding one qualifying word to a URL is the cheapest ranking move available
to you.
T O O B R O A D
/sunscreen
Competing with national brands and
marketplaces. Page 900.
W I N N A B L E
/best-sunscreen-for-oily-skin-in-india
Smaller index, real search volume, matches intent.
If you want to know how competitive a term is, search it and read the slugs of the top five
results. The answer is sitting in plain sight in the address bar.
T H E F A I L U R E M O D E
You can narrow too far. A slug nobody searches will rank first and bring nothing. Ranking
number one for a phrase with no volume is not a win, it is a page you wasted an afternoon
on. Narrow until the fight is winnable, then stop.
While you are in there: take your brand name out of page titles
Keep it on pricing, contact and support. Everywhere else it dilutes the title and competes with
your own homepage for branded clicks. If your brand name contains a generic word, it also
quietly creates cannibalisation you will struggle to diagnose later.

05
Keyword domains still work 
HANDLE WITH CARE
Exact match domains were devalued. They were not removed.
The example from the podcast is instructive. To compete against companies ten times their size,
they bought freeloadbalancer.com for the price of a coffee, put a cheap theme on it, and
shipped. It produced 600 leads in eight weeks and roughly 160 a month after that. A second site
built the same way turned into a client worth a quarter of a million dollars a year.
W H Y I T W O R K E D , A N D T H I S I S T H E W H O L E P O I N T
That site was a real product. An actual free, throughput limited load balancer that people
downloaded and used. It had standalone value, its own reason to exist, and its own
audience.
What does not work, and what will eventually get you classified as a doorway site, is buying five
keyword domains, filling them with thin blog posts, and funnelling everything to your main site.
That pattern is named in Google's spam policy. The distinction is not how many domains you
own. It is whether each one is a real thing.
B E F O R E Y O U B U Y T E N D O M A I N S
Cheap first year pricing is not the cost. Renewals are several times the promotional rate,
every domain needs its own content and its own maintenance, and the podcast example
took eight weeks to produce results with a real product behind it. Budget for the second
year before you buy the first.
06
Stop disavowing backlinks
Spammy looking links are not the same as link spam. Google is looking for unnatural networks
and link farms, and it now tends to zero those links rather than penalise the site receiving them.
Appearing on low quality blogs does nothing to you.
The disavow tool is for one situation: you bought links, you know exactly which ones, and you
are cleaning up after yourself. If you cannot name the links, you are guessing, and disavowing
your own good links is a real way to make things worse.
Negative SEO is also much harder to execute than the panic suggests. Done incorrectly it can
help the target rather than hurt them.

07
Clicks matter. Dwell time probably does not.
CONTESTED
Flagged honestly, because credible people disagree about this one.
The argument against dwell time as a ranking factor is practical. Blog pages bounce at close to
100 percent by nature, because people read the answer and leave. That is a satisfied visit, not a
failed one. Someone may read ten pages, disappear for six months, come back and convert in
nine seconds. Punishing the site for that pattern would make no sense.
Click through rate in the results page is different, because that data is entirely inside Google's
control. If a lower result consistently earns a better than average click through rate, the order
changes. Pogo sticking, going back and picking a different result, is real for the same reason.
W H E R E T H E D I S A G R E E M E N T S I T S
Other respected practitioners point to material from the antitrust trial suggesting Google
may use this data after all. Treat this section as one experienced reading rather than settled
fact, and do not rebuild your content strategy around either position.
08
Publish first, judge later
The method that produces volume is uncomfortable if you are a perfectionist. Pick a hundred
topics with low competition. Publish them. Roughly seventy will land. Now you have a much
easier job, because the thirty that did not are in the same topic clusters as the seventy that did,
and a few internal links from the winners will usually pull them up.
The alternative, reviewing every page before it goes live, produces better individual pages and
far fewer of them. Nobody can predict which page ranks. The data can, once the page exists.
T H E M I N D S E T
Google is not watching every move you make. You can publish a page, see it fail, take it
down and try again. Reduce the number of things on your checklist until only the ones that
move the needle remain, then stop adding to it.

09
Clarity beats cleverness
Two stories about ugly pages that made money.
A landing page review show once pulled up Salesforce's page. Three bullet points, an
unattractive form, everything crammed top left. The room wanted to tear it apart. The host
stopped them, because he knew the team had A/B tested it a thousand times and it converted.
The second is better. To compete against two competitors charging eight to ten times more, a
founder spent a weekend building a single page that was nothing but a comparison table. No
logos, no navigation, no design. Both competitors' full product ranges, listed against theirs, with
the prices neither competitor published anywhere.
W H Y I T W O R K E D
Google surfaced the top of the table directly in the results, so the searcher saw all three
names and the price gap before clicking. And the page had already done the buyer's
homework. They needed to walk into a finance meeting with a comparison, and the page
handed them one.
What this means for your pages
Put the answer at the top. One person reported a 33 percent conversion lift from adding a
summary line above the fold, and the reason is not sophisticated: people were leaving before
finding the answer. Go deeper further down for whoever wants it.
Watch a session recording some time. People do not read your carefully constructed argument.
They scan for the specific thing they came for, and if they cannot find it quickly they leave. The
most effective ads in one B2B account were little more than lists of acronyms, because the buyer
was ticking off requirements and nothing else.
T H E U N C O M F O R T A B L E V E R S I O N
If your founder says "that should be on the landing page", it usually already is. People are
not reading it. That is a clarity problem, not a content problem, and adding more words
makes it worse.

D O T H I S W E E K
The short version
Nine moves is a lot to hold. These six are the ones you can start on today, in
order.
I F Y O U D O O N L Y O N E
Do the capture and the triage. It costs an afternoon, it needs no tools and no budget, and it
produces a build list that is correct rather than merely long.
Checklist
11
Run the capture. Search your five main keywords. Expand every People Also Ask box. Write
down every question. Expect roughly four per query.
Triage before you build. Reject anything your buyer would not ask. Fold anything that
belongs on a page you already have. Absorb genuine duplicates. Build what is left.
Check Search Console before each new page. Clicks on the target page mean add H2s.
Zero clicks mean build the new page.
Find your stuck pages. Filter Search Console for crawled and not indexed. Republish the
ones that matter at a narrower URL and 301 the old one.
Narrow one slug. Take your most ambitious page, the one that has never ranked, and add
two qualifying words to its URL.
Strip your brand name from every page title except pricing, contact and support.
