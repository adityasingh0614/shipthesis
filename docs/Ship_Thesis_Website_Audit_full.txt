Critical problems (fix before sending any outreach)
1. Your proof contradicts itself across pages
The homepage says Assess Yourself is "Delivered, launching soon." The MVP page says
"10,000+ Verified Users" and "approved on both iOS and Android stores within 4
weeks." The Flutter page says "100% App Store Approval." Those can't all be true.
The AI page lists Poststeady as "Client: Poststeady," but the homepage says it's "Our
own product." Showing your own product as client work looks dishonest when
someone clicks through.
Fix: use only numbers you can show proof for. Show your own products as your own.
2. "Zero hallucinations" in the H1, title tag and meta description
No LLM-based system can promise this. A technical US buyer will read it as a red flag, and
"100% Verified Data Accuracy" and "zero metric hallucinations" add to it. Your actual
approach is good: numbers are computed in code, and the model only writes around them.
Say that instead.
3. Unprovable numbers
"Reduce API bills by up to 70%," "sub-100ms response times," "saving ~50% engineering
overhead," "zero technical debt," "0 ms recovery data loss." Cut them or replace them with
real numbers from your projects.
4. Timelines disagree
The homepage FAQ says MVPs take 6 to 10 weeks. The MVP and SaaS pages and the
outreach plan say 4 to 6. Assess Yourself took 3 to 4. Pick one headline number and
explain the range ("4 to 6 weeks for a tightly scoped MVP; larger scopes take longer").
Cross-site issues
Templated pages. The three newer pages reuse the same section intros word for word
("Built for specific founder needs," "Everything you need to launch with confidence").
On the SaaS and AI pages the FAQ subtitle still says "offline persistence, store
approval," which doesn't apply. It looks copy-pasted, and near-identical text can hurt
your search ranking.

FAQ
How do you stop the AI from making things up?
No model can promise it will never be wrong, so we design around that. Numbers are
calculated in regular code first. The model only writes the explanation around those exact
figures, and we check its output against a strict format and test it before release.
Will our data be used to train AI models?
Not on the business plans we use. [Confirm with each provider's current terms before
publishing.]
How do you manage API costs?
