01 · The brief

A laptop screen that stays how you set it

Calibration report · ChromaLayerSubject: built-in laptop screens
Why we built it

Changing colours with Intel's own software was frustrating, and it never gave full control over the screen. We wanted one tool that works across laptops, not just one brand of graphics.

What we observed

Colours look washed out or too yellow, Windows has no proper colour controls for the built-in screen, and the settings that do exist quietly reset after a restart.

What shipped

Seven colour controls and five presets, from one small window in the system tray, re-applied after every restart, sleep, sign-in and display change.

  • 7colour controls, combined into one
  • 5ready-made presets
  • 9re-checks in the 25 seconds after each sign-in
  • 0admin rights or drivers needed

    03 · The fight for your screen

    Sign in, and Windows resets you

    The hard part wasn't changing the colours. It was keeping them after Windows quietly undoes them.

    ● VIVID APPLIED · 0 s
    1. 0 s

      You sign in

      Your colours go on the moment the desktop appears, then get checked again a quarter-second and one second later.

    2. 15-25 s

      Windows quietly resets them

      Some time between 15 and 25 seconds after you sign in, Windows rebuilds part of its display pipeline and wipes the colours. Most tools lose here.

    3. Every ½ s

      The app notices

      For the first 30 seconds it checks twice a second whether the screen still shows your settings. After that, every 5 seconds.

    4. 25 s

      Colours back, and they hold

      Nine planned re-checks cover the whole window, after every sign-in, unlock, wake from sleep and display change.

    04 · The hard parts

    Lab notes

    A colour tool that breaks your screen is worse than no tool. These are the three things it could never get wrong.

    • Greys stay grey

      Observation
      Boosting colour usually tints whites and greys too.
      Fix
      The vibrancy control is built so black, grey and white never shift, however far you push it.
      Result
      Richer colour, clean whites.

    • Restore always works

      Observation
      If a colour app crashes, you can be stuck with a strange screen.
      Fix
      One shortcut, the tray menu or the main window puts the original colours back, and they're restored even if the app crashes.
      Result
      You can always get back.

    • Startup never waits

      Observation
      The window used to wait up to 10 seconds on a licence check.
      Fix
      Startup no longer waits on the network, and if the first colour change fails, it tries again up to 5 times.
      Result
      Colours on as soon as you sign in.

    05 · Judgement

    What we left out, on purpose

    • No overlay, no driver

      It uses the same built-in Windows feature as its own accessibility colour filters, so it needs no admin rights and installs nothing deep in the system.

    • One screen, done well

      No HDR, multiple monitors or ARM laptops in version one. The built-in screen had to be right first.

    • No health claims

      No "eye health", no "calibrated". It makes the screen look better, and says only that.

    Under the hood

    How it's built, in plain words.
    1. The tray app
      WPF
      The window, the sliders and the presets.
    2. Settings and licensing
      Application layer
      Saves your profile safely and runs the trial.
    3. The colour maths
      ColorEngine · one 5×5 matrix
      Turns seven sliders into one change. No Windows code, so it's fully testable.
    4. The screen
      Windows Magnification API
      Applies the change, watched by the watchdog and the sign-in and wake monitor.
    • Licence check · Cloudflare Workers
    • Updates · GitHub Releases + Velopack
    1. One combined change per adjustment. Seven controls never fight each other. (single colour matrix)
    2. Colour maths kept apart from Windows. It can be tested on its own, and moved to another platform later.
    3. Never block startup on the network. Your colours don't wait for a server.
    4. Settings saved in one step. A crash mid-save can't leave a half-written file. (atomic write)
